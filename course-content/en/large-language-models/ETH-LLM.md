# ETH Zürich: Large Language Models

## Course Overview

- University: ETH Zurich
- Prerequisites: No formal prerequisites, but students are expected to be comfortable with probability theory, linear algebra, computational complexity, and machine learning
- Programming Language: Python
- Course Difficulty: 🌟🌟🌟🌟🌟
- Estimated Study Hours: 150 hours

ETH Zürich’s Spring 2025 course starts from the formal foundations of language models and works up to the systems and applications that dominate current practice. The first half covers probabilistic definitions, n-gram and recurrent models, the Transformer, tokenisation, and decoding. The second half moves through transfer learning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, in-context learning and instruction tuning, multimodality, retrieval-augmented generation, reinforcement learning for reasoning, RLHF, and security and privacy.

The course is unusually balanced between theory and implementation. Students write three assignments — one theoretical and two practical — and the final exam is worth half the grade. Public course notes accompany the lectures, which makes it a strong choice for self-learners who want the formal “why” behind LLM behaviour rather than another API tutorial.
